Let a column say what the footer says about it. `.summarise("sum", "avg")` on any column, with four words and no fifth: `count`, `sum`, `avg`, `range`. A list response carries the answers by column path, and the table draws them as lines under the column that asked, in the order it named them.

Worked out by the database over every row a filter left rather than over the page, because a total of the twenty-five rows on screen moves when somebody turns one. The footer and the table above it read the same narrowing, so a total over rows nobody listed is not reachable rather than unlikely. Every footer in a table is one query however many columns asked, which is what the port's named aggregations exist for.

Three readings are decided rather than left to chance. A total of a money column reads as money, through the same formatting a cell uses; a count of that same column does not, a count being a number of rows and not an amount. And no value reads as no value and never as nought: a sum over rows holding nothing in that column has no total, and printing 0 would state one nobody worked out. A footer is the one place that difference cannot be taken back.

A column a reader may not have is never asked about, so the number does not exist to leak: four readings of a filtered total narrow one salary down. What a column cannot bear is refused at boot, naming the column.

Additive. Nothing to do on upgrade unless you want a footer.
